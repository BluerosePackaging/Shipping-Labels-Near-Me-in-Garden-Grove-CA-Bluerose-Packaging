## Shipping Labels for Cartons, Mailers, and Warehouse Packages

Labels must work with the packaging they are applied to. Corrugated boxes, poly mailers, and other surfaces may require different adhesive characteristics to maintain contact during transportation.

Apply labels to a clean, dry, relatively flat surface whenever possible. Avoid placing critical shipping information across box seams, folds, or areas where the label may wrinkle. Keep barcodes clear of tape, heavy creases, and other obstructions that could interfere with scanning.

For shipments exposed to heat, moisture, cold, or extended storage, choose label materials and printing methods suited to those conditions. Direct thermal labels are often appropriate for short-term shipping, while thermal transfer options may be more suitable when printed information needs greater durability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What are shipping labels used for?
Shipping labels display important shipment information, such as delivery addresses, tracking numbers, and barcodes. They help identify packages during dispatch, transportation, and delivery.

### 2. What is the difference between direct thermal and thermal transfer labels?
Direct thermal labels use heat-sensitive material and generally do not require a ribbon. Thermal transfer labels use a ribbon to produce the printed image and can offer greater durability when the material and ribbon are matched to the application.

### 3. Are shipping labels available in roll and fanfold formats?
Both formats are used in shipping operations. The right choice depends on printer compatibility, available storage, and how labels are loaded and printed.

### 4. Can shipping labels be used on corrugated boxes?
Yes. Shipping labels are commonly applied to corrugated cartons. Choose an appropriate adhesive and apply the label to a clean, dry surface for better contact.

### 5. What label size should I choose?
The required size depends on the shipping information, barcode layout, printer, and packaging dimensions. Check your carrier or shipping software requirements before selecting a size.

### 6. Can shipping labels include barcodes?
Yes. Shipping labels commonly display tracking and routing barcodes. Print quality, contrast, label placement, and barcode dimensions all affect scan reliability.

### 7. Are shipping labels suitable for cold or damp conditions?
Some label materials and adhesives are designed for challenging environments, but standard options may not perform well under every condition. Specify the temperature and moisture exposure when choosing labels.

### 8. How can I prevent shipping labels from peeling off?
Use an adhesive suited to the packaging surface, apply the label to a clean and dry area, and press it down firmly. Avoid uneven surfaces, dust, moisture, and placement over seams.

### 9. Can I order shipping labels for regular business use?
Businesses can inquire about quantities and formats that fit their shipping volume. Share your expected usage and printer requirements when requesting a quote.

### 10. What information is needed to request a shipping label quote?
Provide the label size, printing method, supply format, printer details, adhesive requirements, quantity, and intended application. These details help determine which options may suit your operation.
